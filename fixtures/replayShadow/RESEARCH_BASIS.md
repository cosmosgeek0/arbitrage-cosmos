# RUN-17 research basis

Status: `SHADOW_SPECULATIVE`. Official sources and landed owner interfaces rechecked on 2026-07-16 for the port phase. This note explains fixture choices; it is not candidate evidence or an acceptance claim.

- [Node.js 22.23.1 test runner](https://nodejs.org/download/release/latest-v22.x/docs/api/test.html) and [performance hooks](https://nodejs.org/download/release/latest-v22.x/docs/api/perf_hooks.html): focused tests use the stable built-in runner. Corpus timestamps keep injected logical time, source wall time, process identity, and external monotonic measurement separate. The packet does not use mocked or real timers.
- [Binance Spot WebSocket streams](https://github.com/binance/binance-spot-api-docs/blob/master/web-socket-streams.md) and the [official changelog](https://github.com/binance/binance-spot-api-docs/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md): current public-stream behavior includes bounded control-message rates, ping/pong obligations, a `serverShutdown` event, and 24-hour connection rotation. The June 2026 changelog removed a fixed lead-time promise for `serverShutdown`, so the tape records notice occurrence without assuming advance warning. RUN-17 represents all of this as offline observations, never as a live transport.
- [Binance user-data stream](https://github.com/binance/binance-spot-api-docs/blob/master/user-data-stream.md): current delivery wraps events with subscription identity, while `executionReport` still distinguishes execution type, order status, last quantity, cumulative quantity, identifiers, and transaction time. The synthetic ambiguity tape preserves conflicting or missing observations instead of deciding what occurred; it contains no subscription endpoint, API key, or private-stream client.
- [Binance Spot REST API](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/products/spot/rest-api#limits): rate-limit observations preserve status, declared interval/weight, and an explicitly normalized millisecond retry-after fact without implementing a retry policy or asserting a current account-specific limit. The fixture does not claim to preserve the raw header representation, whose documented unit is seconds.
- [Binance public-data repository](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data): timestamps require explicit units, Spot archive timestamps are microseconds from 2025-01-01, and archived data may be corrected. RUN-17 fixtures therefore declare units and provenance and contain no imported live archive bytes.
- [RFC 6455](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6455): a missing close frame is different from an observed clean close, local and remote close observations can differ, and abnormal reconnects should back off. The connection tape separates those facts and uses explicit deterministic delays rather than randomness.
- [AWS Builders' Library retry guidance](https://builder.aws.com/content/3EumjoZascWd1oZiEgL8ORlv3qE/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter): retries can amplify overload; reproducible per-host jitter is preferable for deterministic campaigns. The restart tape carries an explicit delay sequence and never implements retry policy. Its ninth attempt is retained as a hostile policy-violation observation: the landed Binance socket owner caps construction attempts at eight, so this tape cannot be cast into that owner or treated as its policy.
- [Grafana k6 soak guidance](https://grafana.com/docs/k6/latest/testing-guides/test-types/soak-testing/), [scenario model](https://grafana.com/docs/k6/latest/using-k6/scenarios/), and [open/closed workload models](https://grafana.com/docs/k6/latest/using-k6/scenarios/concepts/open-vs-closed/): smoke, average-load, stress, and extended soak are distinct workload shapes. RUN-17 defines fixed virtual arrival schedules for load/soak, a closed-loop latency definition, and a six-hour ramp/plateau/ramp profile, but adds no k6 dependency, runner, or threshold.
- [AWS chaos experiment planning](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/chaos-engineering-on-aws/sample-planning.html): an experiment timeline should separate steady state, fault injection, impact, recovery, known-state verification, and stop. Recovery tapes keep these as requested observations without claiming recovery succeeded.

Each compiled iteration references one complete dataset tape. Dataset `atOffsetMs` values are an isolated injected virtual timeline inside that tape and never consume host measurement time. For `OPEN_ARRIVAL_RATE`, campaign phase offsets are evenly spaced, inclusive, across the phase window. For `CLOSED_LOOP`, frames have no scheduled offset: a frame waits for its prior same-subject frame, when one exists, and for the prior frame assigned to its deterministic global admission slot, when one exists. Admission slots are `phaseOrdinal % maxInFlight`, so at most `maxInFlight` frames can be admitted; `iterations` is a materialization budget, and a future runner must stop at the earlier of that budget or the phase-duration boundary. Dataset and subject assignment are deterministic phase-ordinal round robin. A scheduled fault is bound to that frame's subject and to `BEFORE_TAPE_DISPATCH_FOR_FRAME_SUBJECT`. Recovery fault frames bind the recovery template through frame campaign, fault, and correlation references; non-fault recovery frames cannot select that template. The compiler binds the validated corpus through a canonical SHA-256 content digest and materializes immutable reference frames only: it does not play events, scale or sleep for tape time, honor concurrency, inject faults, open a connection, or measure a host.

The campaign window schedules frame admission, not tape completion. Some complete tapes extend beyond their campaign window, and the six-hour soak definition exceeds the landed capacity owner's bounded execution duration. Any future runner must ratify explicit drain, cutoff, and tail-accounting semantics through the existing owners before executing; these definitions alone are not runnable capacity policy or host evidence. Raw `UNKNOWN` completeness and nullable/string fill facts remain fixture observations. The packet validator's numeric timestamp envelope is intentionally generic rather than a Foundation adapter; a future adapter must enforce Foundation's UTC ceiling and positive maximum-age rule, map `UNKNOWN` to `UNAVAILABLE`, and must not cast raw fields directly into Foundation timestamp or fill contracts.

Rate-limit observations identify their independent fake-provider scope (`REST_REQUEST_WEIGHT_1M` versus `STREAM_CONTROL_MESSAGES_1S`) and the documentation snapshot used to define them. Recovery events label the canonical fact owner for each observation; System Integration is composition only and does not replace Product Assurance, Foundation, Intelligence Runtime, persistence, or Ledger truth.

Limits, endpoints, schemas, and tool behavior can change. Sources must be rechecked again before any later adaptation or execution. Heavy execution remains `DEFERRED_POST_C2_OTHER_MACHINE`.
